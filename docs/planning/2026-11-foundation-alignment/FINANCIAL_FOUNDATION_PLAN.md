# Financial Foundation plan (spec 07)

17 November 2026. For Collins to approve before anything is built.

Spec: `07_Financial_Foundation_Specification_v1.0.md`. Its own words set the scope: "This is not a full accounting product. It defines canonical financial facts generated by Ascend operations." So this plan builds facts, not books: no chart of accounts, no double entry, no journals. An accountant can build those from the facts later (section 13 of the spec, accountant export).

Tax is not in spec 07. Tax rules belong to country configuration (spec 00 section 10) and each transaction keeps the tax rule applied (spec 02). It is a separate piece of work that still waits on the accountant, and nothing here depends on it.

| Receivable | `payment_obligation`: original amount, paid, outstanding, due date (0082, 0086) |

| Location Attribution | `commercial_transaction.location_id` (19 of 20 on live; the one without is an invoice) |

### Money out is scattered

| Spec 07 concept | Where it lives now |

### A second set of books, still read in two places

`ledger_entry` (0004) is written directly by POS, Shop, Services, Documents and Office, across 14 migrations, beside the commerce core. It is the same situation Phase 2 resolved for screens, and it has the same faults:

- **Credit notes are recorded as refunds.** On live, the GHS 1,400 of invoices issued is cancelled by GHS 1,400 of "refund" entries. None has a payment against it: one is credit note CRN-2026-0001 (GHS 400), and the other two (GHS 300 and GHS 700) point to Adepa Online's credit notes, whose documents were later deleted. No money was paid back.
- **Invoice revenue has no location** (the 2 document entries on live).
- **Its kinds are not the spec's.** It has no idea of money in versus revenue earned, verification level, counterparty or reversal reference.

Who still reads it:

1. **Shared reports to institutions** (0023, `revenue_summary`). A lender sees monthly revenue and refunds from `ledger_entry`, so the credit notes above reach a lender as refunds.
2. **`business_revenue`** view (0017). Nothing in the app reads it.

The old `receivable` table (0004) is the same story: written by the products beside `payment_obligation`. The **MOT review** (0024, the collections check) still counts overdue invoices from it, and the reconciliation reports compare against it.

## 2. What the spec asks for, and what this plan builds

### 2.1 One financial event stream (spec sections 3, 4, 5)

A new append-only table, `financial_event`, one row per financial fact, with exactly the spec's fields: business, event id, source, source transaction, amount, currency, direction (in or out), kind, category, location, counterparty, when it happened, verification level, and what it reverses.

The kinds keep two things apart that the old ledger mixes:

| Kind | Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| revenue_earned | in | a sale, fulfilled order, completed booking or issued invoice became earned (accrual) |
| money_received | in | a payment arrived (cash) |
| refund_paid | out | money given back |
| credit_given | out | revenue taken back without money moving (credit note) |
| reversal | out | a sale undone at the till |
| expense | out | an Office expense |
| till_expense | out | money taken out of the drawer during a shift |
| provider_fee | out | what the payment provider kept |
| settlement_received | in | the provider paid the merchant out |

The rows are projected from the operational records by the same pattern as the commerce core: database triggers that can never block a sale, an order or an expense (a failure is logged, like `commerce_projection_error`), plus a one-off backfill. Products do not write `financial_event` themselves.

Verification is the five-level ladder from Phase 5: cash is merchant-declared, a provider-confirmed payment is payment-verified.

### 2.2 Categories (section 11)

`financial_category`: a standard set per country (Ghana first), plus categories a business adds for itself. Expenses and till expenses carry a category; revenue is categorised by where it came from (till, shop, services, invoices). I will draft the standard Ghana list (stock purchases, rent, transport, utilities, airtime and data, salaries and wages, MoMo and bank charges and so on) for Collins to edit. It is a business list, not an accounting one; it does not need the accountant.

### 2.3 Receivables with aging (section 6)

A view over `payment_obligation`: original amount, paid, outstanding, due date, days overdue, aging bucket (not yet due, 1 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, over 90 days) and status. The MOT collections check moves to it.

### 2.4 Cash position (section 8)

One function, five figures kept apart, never added together into one number that pretends to be more than it is:

- cash recorded (merchant-declared cash in, less cash paid out)
- verified digital payments
- pending settlement (paid but not yet settled to the merchant)
- outstanding receivables
- known payables (0 until procurement exists, and shown as "not tracked yet", not as 0)

Profit is not shown here. Section 8 says to avoid presenting profit where the data is insufficient; a profit estimate belongs to the Finance product, gated on completeness (see decision 6).

### 2.5 Reconciliation (section 9)

Most of this exists: shift counting (0013), payments against obligations (0084), settlement (0092). Added here: a check that every financial event matches its source and every source has its events, in the shape of the Phase 2 reconciliation report, so the switch in 2.8 is made on evidence. Bank reconciliation is "later" in the spec and stays later.

### 2.6 Periods and change control (section 12)

`financial_period`: one per business per month, open or closed, with who closed it and when. Once closed, nothing dated in that month changes. Activity that arrives late (a till that was offline, an invoice dated back) still keeps its real date, and is reported in the first open period with a note that it belongs to an earlier one (decision 3).

### 2.7 Accountant export (section 13)

A CSV of a period's financial events, one row each, with category, counterparty, location and verification, built from `financial_event` alone. A screen to download it can follow with the Finance product; the export function comes first.

### 2.8 Retire the second set of books

Once the reconciliation report is clean on live:

1. Shared reports to institutions read their revenue summary from `financial_event`. Credit notes then reach a lender as credits, not refunds.
2. The MOT collections check reads the receivables view.
3. The products stop writing `ledger_entry` and `receivable`. Both stay, readable and unedited, as history.

This is the Phase 2 order: readers first, then writers, never both at once.

## 3. Sequence, with exit gates

| Step | What | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | `financial_event` and the money-in projection from the core (revenue earned, money received, refunds, credits, reversals), with backfill | Every earned transaction, payment and adjustment on live has its events; totals match the core by hand on a throwaway business |
| F2 | Money out: expenses, till expenses, provider fees, settlements; `financial_category` with the standard Ghana list | An expense, a till paid-out and a categorised business-defined expense each produce one event, on live |
| F3 | Receivables aging, cash position, and the reconciliation report | The report shows every source and every event accounted for, on live |
| F4 | Periods and change control | A late till sale for a closed month keeps its date and lands in the open period, on a throwaway business |
| F5 | Accountant export | A period's CSV totals match the cash position and the receivables view |
| F6 | Readers moved (institution reports, MOT), then `ledger_entry` and `receivable` writes stopped | Shared report revenue matches the core; no product writes the old tables |

F1 to F5 add things and change nothing shown. F6 changes what a lender sees in a shared report, and Collins sees the before and after first, as in 5e.

## 4. How it will be tested

As every phase so far: the local Postgres harness replaying every migration, with worked examples for each kind (a cash sale, an online-paid invoice, a partial refund, a credit note, a reversal, an expense, a till paid-out, a late offline sale into a closed month); then live checks on throwaway ZZ businesses, cleaned up by id; then the reconciliation report on live.

## 5. Risks

- **Two sets of money-out records disagree.** `ledger_entry` already holds expenses. Mitigation: `financial_event` reads the operational tables (`expense`, `till_expense`), not the ledger, and the reconciliation report compares the two before F6.
- **A projection trigger blocks a sale.** Mitigation: the commerce core's pattern, where a projection failure is recorded and never raised.
- **Over-reading the cash position.** Mitigation: five figures, never summed into one, and "not tracked yet" where Ascend has no data.
- **Shared reports change.** Mitigation: F6 is last, with the before and after list first.

## 6. Out of scope here

- Tax computation (country configuration and the commerce core; waits on the accountant).
- Payables and procurement (spec section 7: future).
- Bank reconciliation (spec: later).
- The Finance product screens (Phase 6). This plan gives them their data.
- Evidence from financial events (spec section 15). Commerce evidence already comes from the core (5f); expenses already earn documentation evidence through Office. Nothing changes until the Finance product gives a reason.

## 7. Decisions for Collins

1. **One `financial_event` stream projected from operations**, with products never writing it themselves. *Recommended.* The alternative, repairing `ledger_entry`, keeps products writing money facts in 14 places.
2. **Retire `ledger_entry` and `receivable` in F6**, after the reconciliation report is clean and you have seen the before and after for shared reports. *Recommended.*
3. **Late activity for a closed month** keeps its real date and is reported in the first open period, marked as belonging to the earlier one. *Recommended.* The alternative, refusing it, would lose real sales a till recorded offline.
4. **The standard Ghana category list**: I draft it in F2, you edit it. *Recommended.*
5. **An invoice with no location** is attributed to the business's first location when it has only one, and shown as "not attributed" when it has several. *Recommended.* The alternative, always the first location, would put a branch's invoices on head office.
6. **No profit figure in the foundation.** Cash position shows five separate figures; a profit estimate waits for the Finance product, shown only when the period has expenses recorded and no unreconciled shifts. *Recommended*, because section 8 asks for exactly this restraint.
