1. Executive Summary
Ascend Services is the AscendSME operating set for businesses that sell time, expertise, appointments, sessions, scheduled work, field visits or projects. It replaces fragmented WhatsApp chats, notebooks, personal calendars and informal payment follow-up with a simple service operating system designed for Ghanaian and African businesses.
The product must not assume that every service is a fixed salon appointment. It must support six primary operating models: fixed time-slot booking, request-to-book, quote-before-confirmation, classes and group sessions, field or home services, and project-based engagements.
Ascend Services will provide an immediate customer-facing booking or enquiry page while quietly creating structured operational evidence from confirmed appointments, deposits, completed work, repeat customers, fulfilment, cancellations and verified payments.
Strategic roleAscend Services completes AscendSME’s three principal customer-facing entry points: Ascend POS for walk-in commerce, Ascend Shop for online product commerce, and Ascend Services for bookable or requested work.

2. Product Vision and Positioning
Vision: Make it simple for any African service business to become bookable, organised, paid and credible.
Positioning: Ascend Services is a lightweight service operating system that helps businesses publish services, manage availability, confirm work, collect deposits, communicate with customers and document service revenue.
Primary promise: Customers can book or request the service. The business can manage the work from enquiry to completion. AscendSME captures reliable evidence of business activity in the process.
Audience

3. Problem Definition
Service businesses often receive enquiries through WhatsApp, calls and social media with no structured path to confirmation.
Availability is held in memory or personal calendars, increasing double booking and missed work.
Deposits and outstanding balances are tracked manually and are difficult to reconcile.
Providers lose time answering repetitive questions about price, location, duration and availability.
Customers receive inconsistent confirmations, reminders and cancellation information.
Staff calendars, service locations and shared resources are difficult to coordinate.
Custom and project-based services do not fit rigid appointment software.
Completed work and service revenue leave little structured evidence for financial reporting or readiness.
4. Goals and Non-Goals
Product goals
Enable a provider to publish a credible service page in one focused session.
Support multiple service operating models without cluttering the first-use experience.
Reduce manual scheduling, confirmation and payment follow-up.
Make deposits, balances, cancellations and rescheduling understandable.
Create shared customer, payment, document and evidence records across AscendSME.
Support low-literacy, mobile-first and weak-connectivity environments.
Create monetizable value through subscriptions, bookings, messaging, promotion, assisted setup and institutional programmes.
Non-goals for the initial release
Operating as the legal service provider or employer of listed professionals.
Guaranteeing provider quality, attendance or customer outcomes.
Building a full workforce marketplace with central fulfilment.
Providing payroll, advanced accounting or enterprise resource planning inside the Services set.
Automating complex professional estimates without merchant approval.
Supporting emergency dispatch or regulated clinical scheduling in the first release.

7. Product Principles
Value before completeness: publish the first service before requesting full readiness information.
Service language before software language: use Book, Request, Confirm, Start, Complete and Pay.
One clear path per service: customers should not see irrelevant booking options.
Merchant approval is authoritative: AI and automation may suggest, but must not commit custom work without approval.
Deposits are commitments, not hidden charges.
Availability must account for people, locations, resources and travel.
Customer communication must be consistent and consented.
Every meaningful operational event should be auditable.
Private notes and public service information must remain clearly separated.
The product must work for solo providers and small teams without feeling enterprise-heavy.

9. Merchant Onboarding
The default onboarding should move the provider from an empty state to a bookable or requestable service page within minutes.
Choose what customers should do: book a time, request a date, ask for a quote, join a class or request a visit.
Add the first service manually, from a template or with AI-assisted setup.
Add price or choose starting price, quote required or contact for price.
Add duration, capacity or expected response time only where relevant.
Set basic availability or indicate manual confirmation.
Preview the public service page.
Add business or provider name, contact, location and payment preference.
Accept required terms and activate.
Share the booking or request link.

46. Acceptance Scenarios
Scenario 1: Fixed appointment
A customer selects a haircut, available barber and time, pays the required deposit and receives confirmation without creating an account.
Scenario 2: Request-to-book
A makeup customer submits an event date and location. The provider proposes a revised time, and confirmation occurs only after acceptance.
Scenario 3: Quote-before-confirmation
A repair customer uploads issue details. The technician sends a versioned quote that becomes a job after customer acceptance.
Scenario 4: Class capacity
A training session with ten seats stops accepting confirmed registrations after the tenth seat.
Scenario 5: Field service
A cleaning business receives a location-based request, assigns a team, records completion and collects the balance.
Scenario 6: Project engagement
A consultant converts an accepted proposal into milestones and invoices the first milestone.
Scenario 7: Calendar conflict
Two customers try to reserve the last slot simultaneously. Only one confirmation succeeds.
Scenario 8: Deposit retry
A customer retries a payment after a network interruption. One payment and one confirmation are created.
Scenario 9: Cancellation policy
A customer cancels outside the refundable window and sees the configured deposit treatment before confirming.
Scenario 10: No-show
A provider marks a customer no-show with permission. The event affects no-show reporting but does not create payment evidence.
Scenario 11: Progressive onboarding
A solo tutor activates a request page without entering TIN, registration or employee information.
Scenario 12: AI suggestion
AI suggests a service description and duration. The merchant edits both, and the edited version remains authoritative.
Scenario 13: Offline agenda
A provider temporarily loses connectivity and can still view the cached day agenda and pending sync state.
Scenario 14: Private notes
A receptionist can see booking contact information but cannot see restricted service notes.
Scenario 15: Multi-location
A salon service is available at one branch only and does not appear for the other branch.
Scenario 16: Resource conflict
A service requiring the only treatment room cannot overlap another booking using that room.
Scenario 17: Balance payment
A customer pays a deposit online and the remaining balance in cash. The booking and ledger reconcile.
Scenario 18: Discover attribution
A customer arrives from a sponsored service spotlight and completes a booking. Source is retained without changing Trust Level.
Scenario 19: Merchant suspension
A serious complaint removes the provider from Discover but does not silently delete private operational records.
Scenario 20: Institutional cohort
A sponsor funds licences for a provider cohort and sees only authorised aggregate performance.
